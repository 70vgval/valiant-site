VH – VJ Era

Hey Charger!

The fastback that became Australia's most iconic muscle car.

Born in 1971

The VH Charger arrived with a shape unlike anything else on Australian roads. Aggressive fastback lines, bold marketing, and a war cry that echoed from billboards to Bathurst: "Hey Charger!"

Available in base, XL, and 770 trim levels, the Charger range culminated in the homologation specials — the R/T E38 and the legendary R/T E49.

Homologation Special

R/T E38

Built to homologate the Charger for Series Production racing, the E38 was a stripped-back, race-ready weapon. Just 280 were produced.

Powered by the 4.3L (265ci) Hemi-6 with triple Weber carburettors, the E38 produced around 280 bhp. A Borg-Warner four-speed, Sure-Grip diff, and heavy-duty suspension completed the package.

The Holy Grail

R/T E49

Only 149 E49s were built. It remains the most collectable Australian muscle car ever made — and for good reason.

The E49 took the E38 formula further: a blueprinted 4.3L Hemi-6 with triple 45mm Weber carburettors pushing 302 bhp. It was the fastest accelerating production car in Australia at the time, capable of 14.4 seconds in the quarter mile.

Today, surviving E49s command seven-figure prices at auction — a testament to their rarity and racing pedigree.

VJ Generation

E55 340 V8

For the 1973 VJ Charger, Chrysler took a different approach — American muscle in Australian sheet metal.

The E55 packed a 340 cubic inch (5.6L) LA-series V8, producing 275 bhp. Unlike the Hemi-6 homologation cars, the E55 was a production model aimed at buyers who wanted V8 thunder rather than six-cylinder fury.
